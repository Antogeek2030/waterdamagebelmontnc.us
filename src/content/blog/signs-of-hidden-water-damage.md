Not every water problem announces itself with a visible puddle. Slow leaks, minor roof intrusion, and elevated humidity in crawl spaces can go unnoticed until secondary effects appear. Catching these signs early often keeps the repair smaller and less disruptive.

## Musty or earthy odors

A persistent musty smell, especially in closets, basements, or near exterior walls, can indicate moisture supporting microbial growth. The odor may be stronger after humid weather or when the HVAC runs.

## Discoloration or soft spots

Brownish or yellowish stains on ceilings and walls are classic clues. Soft or spongy drywall, baseboards that feel damp, or flooring that buckles or cups also point to moisture that needs attention.

## Peeling paint or warped trim

Paint that bubbles or peels, wallpaper that loosens, or wood trim that warps or separates from the wall can result from water migrating through materials. These symptoms sometimes appear well after the original leak has slowed or stopped.

## Elevated humidity or condensation

Windows that fog excessively, or rooms that feel consistently damper than the rest of the house, may signal a moisture source nearby—sometimes in a wall cavity, under a floor, or in a poorly ventilated crawl space.

## Higher utility bills without explanation

In some cases, a hidden leak increases water usage. Comparing recent bills to the same period in prior years can reveal unusual patterns worth investigating.

## What to do if you notice these signs

Start by checking obvious sources: under sinks, around toilets, near the water heater, and at appliance connections. Look at the attic for damp insulation or staining on the underside of the roof deck.

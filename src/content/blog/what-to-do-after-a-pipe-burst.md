A burst pipe can release a surprising amount of water in a short time. Acting quickly helps limit damage to floors, walls, and belongings. Here is a practical sequence many Belmont homeowners find useful.

## 1. Shut off the water if you can do so safely

Locate your main shutoff valve—often near the water meter, in a basement, crawl space, or utility closet—and turn it off. If you cannot find it or reach it safely, call for help and avoid areas where water and electricity mix.

## 2. Protect yourself and others

Stay out of standing water if electrical outlets or appliances may be involved. Keep children and pets away from the affected area. Use towels or a wet vacuum only if conditions are clearly safe.

## 3. Document the scene

Take photos or short videos of the water, the source if visible, and any damaged materials. This record can help later conversations with insurance or restoration professionals.

## 4. Move what you can

If it is safe, move furniture, rugs, and valuables out of the wet zone. Elevate items on blocks or place them in a dry room. Avoid dragging heavy pieces across saturated floors.

## 5. Call for professional extraction

Household towels and fans help with small spills, but a broken supply line or failed fitting often needs commercial extraction and controlled drying. Prompt professional drying reduces the chance of swelling, staining, and secondary issues such as musty odors.

## What happens after the water is out

Once standing water is gone, moisture often remains in subfloors, wall cavities, and under cabinets. Moisture meters and proper air movement help bring those materials back to normal levels. Rushing to close up wet areas can trap humidity and create later problems.
